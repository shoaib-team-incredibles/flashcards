# How to master French

A complete roadmap from zero to fluent — what to learn, in what order, how many hours it takes, and the daily routine that gets you there.

## The honest timeline

The US Foreign Service Institute rates French as a **Category I** language for English speakers: about **600–750 class hours** to reach professional working proficiency. With a solid self-study routine, that's roughly:

| Level (CEFR) | What you can do | Cumulative hours | At 1 h/day |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** — Beginner | Introduce yourself, order food, ask directions | ~80 h | ~3 months |
| **A2** — Elementary | Talk about your day, past and plans; handle travel | ~200 h | ~7 months |
| **B1** — Intermediate | Hold real conversations, follow slow podcasts and TV with subtitles | ~400 h | ~13 months |
| **B2** — Upper intermediate | Watch films, debate, work in French with effort | ~600 h | ~20 months |
| **C1** — Advanced | Fluent and spontaneous; nuance, humour, idioms | ~900 h+ | ~2.5–3 years |

You don't need talent. You need **hours** and the **right kind** of hours. The fastest learners are simply the most consistent.

## The 5 pillars

Every hour you spend should feed one of these. Skip one and you'll plateau.

1. **Vocabulary** — the 1,000 most common words cover ~85% of everyday speech; 2,000 cover ~90%. This is where flashcards shine.
2. **Grammar** — enough to build sentences, not a linguistics degree. Learn a pattern, then see it in the wild.
3. **Listening (input)** — hours of real French you *mostly* understand. This is what makes you fluent.
4. **Speaking (output)** — talking out loud, early and badly. Fluency is a motor skill.
5. **Pronunciation** — fix it in the first months or you'll carry an accent that makes people switch to English.

## Your daily routine (60 minutes)

| Block | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Review** | 15 min | Do *all* your due cards in KnowDeck — every single day. Add ~15 new words + 3–5 grammar or expression cards. |
| **Input** | 25 min | A podcast, YouTube video or show at your level. Re-listen to the same episode 2–3 times. |
| **Output** | 15 min | Shadow audio, speak to yourself, write 5 sentences, or talk with a tutor/partner. |
| **Grammar** | 5 min | One grammar card or concept; write 3 of your own example sentences with it. |

**Short on time?** Never skip reviews. 15 minutes of cards keeps everything alive; the rest compounds when you have more time.

## Using the three decks

- **French — Popular words (1,000 cards)**: the 1,000 most useful words, ordered by return on your time. The first ~220 are the core of every conversation; after that, verbs, nouns, adjectives and linking words are mixed so each day's new cards are the most valuable ones left. At **15 new a day** you'll finish in about **10 weeks** — and those 1,000 words cover roughly 85% of everyday speech. Say the example sentence **out loud** before flipping.
- **French — Cool expressions (200 cards)**: everyday spoken phrases first (the ones you'll hear daily), then idioms, slang and proverbs. Start from **A2**, at 3–5 new a day. Try to use one in conversation each week.
- **French — Grammar (200 cards)**: one rule per card, ordered from the highest-payoff basics to advanced points. Do 3–5 new a day alongside the words; when a card shows a rule you haven't met yet, write one sentence of your own with it.

**Card tips**

- **Listen to every card.** French cards read themselves aloud when they appear. Press **R** or **Listen** to replay, and tap any French sentence in *italics* on the back to hear it. Repeat it out loud — that's free shadowing practice.
- Read the 💡 notes: they flag irregular forms, false friends, register and the traps English speakers fall into.
- Grade honestly. *Again* if you hesitated on the meaning — it's not a failure, it's the algorithm doing its job.
- When you miss a word twice, **add your own card** with a sentence from your life: *Mon frère habite à Lyon* sticks better than a textbook line.
- Run a **Test** once a week (typed answers, reverse direction) to check you can *produce* the French, not just recognise it.
- Every new word you meet in a podcast or show → make a card. Your personal deck becomes the most valuable one.

## Phase 1 — Foundations (A1, months 0–3)

**Goal:** understand the sound system, the 500 most common words, and the present tense.

- **Pronunciation first (week 1–2).** Learn the nasal vowels (*an, in, on, un*), the French **u** vs **ou**, the throaty **r**, silent final letters and liaison. Watch a pronunciation series and mimic it out loud.
- **Grammar:** articles (*le/la/un/une/du*), gender, subject pronouns, *être / avoir / aller / faire*, regular *-er/-ir/-re* verbs in the present, negation, questions with *est-ce que*.
- **Vocabulary:** the first 500 cards of *Popular words*.
- **Input:** beginner podcasts (e.g. *Coffee Break French*, *InnerFrench* is a step up), *Easy French* street interviews on YouTube with subtitles, children's shows.
- **Output:** say every card out loud. Introduce yourself in a 1-minute monologue. Start a tiny journal: 3 sentences a day.
- **Milestone:** a 5-minute conversation about yourself, your work, your family and your city.

## Phase 2 — Building blocks (A2, months 3–7)

**Goal:** talk about past and future, understand slow native speech.

- **Grammar:** *passé composé* (with *avoir* and *être* — DR & MRS VANDERTRAMP), *imparfait*, the difference between them, *futur proche*, object pronouns (*le, lui, y, en*), reflexive verbs, comparatives.
- **Vocabulary:** finish the 1,000 *Popular words*, then mine new words from what you listen to. Start the *Cool expressions* deck.
- **Input:** *InnerFrench* podcast, *Français Authentique*, *Easy French*, graded readers. Switch Netflix shows you already know to French audio + French subtitles.
- **Output:** book a weekly session with a tutor (italki, Preply) or a language exchange (Tandem, HelloTalk). Tell stories about your weekend — that's past tenses in action.
- **Milestone:** tell a 3-minute story about something that happened to you.

## Phase 3 — Breakthrough (B1, months 7–13)

**Goal:** real conversations without planning every sentence.

- **Grammar:** *futur simple*, *conditionnel* (*je voudrais, j'aimerais*), *si* clauses, relative pronouns (*qui, que, où, dont*), the *subjonctif* after the common triggers (*il faut que, je veux que*).
- **Input:** this is where it gets fun — French YouTubers, *Dix pour cent (Call My Agent!)*, *Lupin*, *Le Bureau des Légendes*, French music with lyrics. Aim for 1 h/day of listening, even passively.
- **Output:** 2 conversations per week. Start **thinking in French** — narrate what you're doing as you do it (*Je fais du café, je cherche mes clés…*).
- **Reading:** *Le Petit Prince*, then simple novels (Simenon's *Maigret*, Anna Gavalda), news at *1jour1actu* or *RFI Français facile*.
- **Milestone:** a 30-minute conversation where you rarely switch to English.

## Phase 4 — Fluency (B2 → C1, months 13+)

**Goal:** comfort in any situation; nuance and register.

- **Grammar:** polish — *plus-que-parfait*, past conditional, full subjunctive, *passé simple* (recognition only, for books), pronoun order with two pronouns.
- **Spoken French:** learn how people *actually* talk — dropped *ne* (*je sais pas*), *on* for *nous*, *ouais*, *t'es*, verlan (*ouf, chelou, relou*), fillers (*du coup, bref, genre, en fait*).
- **Input:** native podcasts (*Transfert*, *Les Pieds sur terre*, *France Culture*), films without subtitles, news (*France Inter*, *Le Monde*), books you'd read in English anyway.
- **Output:** debate opinions, give a 5-minute talk, write emails and short essays and get them corrected.
- **Milestone:** take the **DELF B2** or **DALF C1** — a great forcing deadline.

## Techniques that work

- **Spaced repetition** — what KnowDeck does. Reviewing right before you forget makes memories last years with minimal time.
- **Shadowing** — play a short clip and repeat it *simultaneously*, copying rhythm and intonation. The fastest way to improve accent and speaking speed.
- **Comprehensible input** — listen to material where you understand ~70–90%. Too easy = no growth; too hard = noise.
- **Sentence mining** — when you hear a useful sentence, put the *whole sentence* on a card, not just the word.
- **Output early** — speak from week one, even just to yourself. Mistakes are data.
- **Change your environment** — set your phone to French, follow French accounts, label things in your home, cook from French recipes.
- **Learn chunks, not just words** — *J'ai envie de…*, *Ça fait longtemps que…*, *Je n'arrive pas à…* These ready-made phrases make you fluent faster than conjugation tables.

## Common mistakes English speakers make

- *Je suis 25 ans* ✗ → **J'ai 25 ans** ✓ (age uses *avoir*)
- *Je suis chaud* ✗ (means you're… excited, in the wrong way) → **J'ai chaud** ✓
- *Je suis fini* ✗ → **J'ai fini** ✓
- *J'aime café* ✗ → **J'aime le café** ✓ (French needs the article)
- *Je vais à France* ✗ → **Je vais en France** ✓
- *Il est un médecin* ✗ → **Il est médecin** / **C'est un médecin** ✓
- *Je suis excité* — avoid; it often has a sexual meaning. Say **J'ai hâte** (I can't wait) or **Je suis enthousiaste**.
- *Actuellement* = **currently**, not *actually* (*en fait*). *Librairie* = **bookshop**, not library (*bibliothèque*). *Sensible* = **sensitive**. *Rester* = **to stay**, not rest.
- Pronouncing final letters: *Paris* is *pa-ree*, *vous parlez* is *voo par-lay*.

## Your first week

1. **Day 1:** Add all three decks. Learn the alphabet and the 4 nasal vowels. Do 10 new vocab cards.
2. **Day 2:** Reviews + 10 new words. Watch one *Easy French* video twice.
3. **Day 3:** Grammar cards on articles and gender. Write 3 sentences about yourself.
4. **Day 4:** Reviews + 10 new. Listen to one beginner podcast episode, then shadow 2 minutes of it.
5. **Day 5:** *être* and *avoir* cards. Record a 30-second voice memo introducing yourself.
6. **Day 6:** Change your phone language to French. Reviews + 10 new.
7. **Day 7:** Take a 20-question Test in KnowDeck. Book your first tutor session or language exchange for next week.

> *Petit à petit, l'oiseau fait son nid.* — Little by little, the bird builds its nest. Show up every day and French will come.
